# City of College Station: Consulting Spend Analysis (FY2023 & FY2024)

Market research for our AI consultant capstone project. The goal is to identify where the City of College Station, Texas spends money on outside consultants, which firms receive that money, and what work they perform.

## Key Findings

- The city spent **$7.79M on true consulting** (engineering & design, studies/advisory/audit/legal, technology & cybersecurity) across FY2023 and FY2024 combined.
- True consulting was **$3.31M in FY2023** and **$4.48M in FY2024**.
- **Engineering & Design is the largest consulting pool** ($5.99M over two years), led by street and park design firms.
- **Studies, Advisory, Audit & Legal** is the category most directly addressable by an AI consultant ($282K in FY2023, $623K in FY2024).
- The largest "professional services" line in the city's books is actually **outsourced operations** (paving crews, a library interlocal agreement, collections), not consulting. We separated it so it does not inflate the consulting numbers.

## Methodology

1. Downloaded the city's check register CSVs for FY2023 (39,290 payments) and FY2024 (37,648 payments).
2. Kept professional services and consulting accounts, plus capital-project engineering and architect/engineer accounts.
3. Excluded, per project scope:
   - **Electricity & water:** Electric, Water, Wastewater, and Utility Customer Service funds
   - **Emergency services:** Fire, Emergency Management, and Emergency Operations departments, plus one vendor that appears to be emergency-services billing
   - **Criminal/courts:** Police, Criminal Investigation, Municipal Court, Court Security, and Court Technology
   - **Non-consulting services:** janitorial, tree trimming, towing, lab testing, medical, security, police body cameras, drug testing, jury fees, polygraph, pre-employment screening
4. Grouped the remaining payments into six categories based on account name and department.
5. Mapped renamed departments across years (e.g., "Cyber Security" in FY2023 became "Cybersecurity" in FY2024).
6. Researched the top 30 vendors against public records (City Council records, city blog, state filings).

## Data Limitations

- **Vendor names are truncated** to 5 characters in both files, so most identities require outside research.
- **Payment descriptions are blank**, so each payment's purpose comes only from the account name and department.
- **Raw file totals differ significantly** ($561M in FY2023 vs. $278M in FY2024), so compare category totals rather than raw file totals.
- **Category assignment is a judgment call** based on account and department names.
- **Unverified vendor roles are hypotheses**, not confirmed facts.
